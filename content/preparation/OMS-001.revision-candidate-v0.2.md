## Intended learning outcomes (all candidate, not yet approved)

By the end of the reviewed unit, a learner should be able to:
1. Describe a systematic pre-extraction decision sequence: patient and tooth/site verification; indication/alternative discussion; medical, medication and allergy screening; clinical/radiographic assessment as appropriate; informed consent and documentation.
2. Recognize factors that may change suitability, complexity and referral decisions, including relevant systemic disease, therapies affecting bleeding/healing, anatomy, infection and limited mouth opening.
3. Distinguish immediate bleeding concerns from a stable, low-severity presentation, with red flags requiring urgent/emergency escalation.
4. Identify **which anticoagulant class** is in a case before discussing INR, medication decisions or procedural bleeding risk.
5. Explain uncertainty and safe collaboration with supervising dentist or the patient's prescribing clinician rather than guessing a medication-withholding decision.

Outcomes are not a complete Oral & Maxillofacial Surgery curriculum. Candidate links: BDS-02-01 and BDS-02-03. Local anaesthesia safety (BDS-02-02), odontogenic infection (02-04), trauma (02-05) and jaw pathology (02-06) need *separate, fully sourced modules*. Competent reviewers must review objectives and any clinical claims.

## Five-stage interactive teaching draft

### Stage A — Verify the correct person, site, purpose and consent

A sound extraction assessment starts by checking patient identity, the tooth/site, clinical indication, appropriate investigations and relevant alternatives. Review the health and medication history before planning care. Explain potential benefits, risks, costs and alternatives; check capacity/consent requirements applicable in the treatment setting; document the agreed plan.

**Evidence anchor:** Indian Dental Association (IDA), *For Patient Management*, Standards 9 (medical history and oral assessment), 10 (treatment planning and informed consent), 12 (ongoing care). Source: https://www.ida.org.in/Accreditation/Details/ForPatientManagement

**Checkpoint prompt:** A completed consent form is in the chart, but the medical history and current medications are not documented. What assessment remains incomplete, and why should the operator clarify it before proceeding?

**Hint candidate:** Consent and systemic risk assessment are distinct requirements. The signature does not replace screening.

### Stage B — Focused general and local assessment

Review relevant cardiovascular conditions, infection/antibiotic-prophylaxis considerations *only when indicated by valid current guidance*, glycaemic control, bleeding and healing risks, kidney/liver disease, allergies and therapies such as anticoagulants/antiplatelets and antiresorptives. Examine access, mouth opening, the tooth/periodontium and adjacent anatomy. Decide whether imaging is clinically indicated and interpret it within its limitations. Don't assert that every patient needs the same imaging or laboratory investigation.

**Reference status:** IDA Standard 9 supports individualized history/examination. The particular disease-specific and drug-related checks, radiographic indications, endocarditis prophylaxis rules, antiresorptive-associated risks, contraindications, technique and anaesthesia require verified current *topic-specific* dental guidelines and exact textbook chapters before student release.

**Checkpoint prompt:** Which items from the medical or dental history, physical examination and medication list could change the suitability and planning of extraction?

**Hint candidate:** Think in three categories: patient-level risk, tooth/site complexity and consent/planning.

### Stage C — Plan for bleeding and postoperative monitoring

Before an invasive procedure, classify the **specific procedure's** bleeding risk, medicine use and relevant comorbidities. Where anticoagulants are prescribed, avoid generalizing from one agent to another or assuming that stopping medication is safer than continuing. Consider the scope of local haemostasis, subsequent monitoring and who to contact if bleeding persists. Post-treatment advice must match the patient's situation and local clinical setting.

For a presentation of post-extraction bleeding, assess **severity and systemic stability first**, including concerning airway/systemic symptoms; distinguish mild oozing from brisk or uncontrolled bleeding. SDCEP's March 2026 *Post-extraction haemorrhage* guidance describes firm pressure using moist gauze/cotton for **20 minutes** before checking in the initial care pathway, and subsequent professional management may address the bleeding source, local dressing or suturing. Do not treat the 20-minute step as a reason to delay emergency care for a compromised patient.

**Evidence anchors:**
- SDCEP, *Management of Acute Dental Problems*, 2nd ed. March 2026, “Post-extraction haemorrhage”: https://www.acutedentalproblems.sdcep.org.uk/guidance/management-of-oral-conditions/common-oral-conditions/post-extraction-haemorrhage/
- SDCEP, same edition, “Categories of Care”: https://www.acutedentalproblems.sdcep.org.uk/supporting-tools/dental-practice-guide/categories-of-care/

**Safety caveat:** SDCEP's system of care and emergency telephone numbers are Scottish/UK-specific. A qualified professional must localize referral/emergency pathways for India. This is a case-based professional learning exercise, not instructions for real unsupervised practice.

### Stage D — Identify the anticoagulant before applying a guidance pathway

The patient's statement “I take a blood thinner” is **insufficient** for any precise extraction/medication plan. Clarify agent(s), class (warfarin/other VKA; DOAC; antiplatelet; injectable or combination), reason for therapy, dosing/timing, other medicines, relevant diseases, bleeding history, complexity of planned procedure and ability to obtain help if bleeding occurs.

**Source-bounded nuance for a future reviewed explanation:** SDCEP *Management of Dental Patients Taking Anticoagulants or Antiplatelet Drugs*, 2nd ed. 2022, “Warfarin or another vitamin K antagonist” discusses warfarin/VKA management with an **INR below 4** and testing ideally within **24 hours**, or up to **72 hours** if INR is stable. This is NOT a blanket INR rule for DOACs. SDCEP's DOAC recommendations explicitly say **INR is unsuitable for monitoring DOAC anticoagulation** and use drug- and procedure-specific treatment pathways. A patient may be harmed by unjustified treatment interruption. Do **not** teach automatic medication suspension, bridging, exact dose timing or an individual prescription from this candidate lesson.

**Evidence anchors:**
- SDCEP 2022, “Warfarin or another vitamin K antagonist”: https://companion.sdcep.org.uk/management-of-dental-patients-taking-anticoagulants-or-antiplatelet-drugs/treatment-recommendations/warfarin-or-another-vitamin-k-antagonist
- SDCEP 2022, “Direct oral anticoagulants — Recommendations”: https://companion.sdcep.org.uk/management-of-dental-patients-taking-anticoagulants-or-antiplatelet-drugs/treatment-recommendations/direct-oral-anticoagulants-doacs/recommendations/
- SDCEP 2022, “Drug interactions”: https://companion.sdcep.org.uk/management-of-dental-patients-taking-anticoagulants-or-antiplatelet-drugs/treatment-recommendations/drug-interactions/

**Checkpoint prompt:** Why is it unsafe to apply a warfarin INR threshold to a patient taking apixaban, or to recommend that an unidentified “blood thinner” be stopped?

**Hint candidate:** First identify the drug class; the tests and risks are not interchangeable.

### Stage E — Explain the decision and follow-up

The appropriate conclusion may be that information is inadequate: clarify history, investigate/refer where clinically necessary, obtain informed consent and arrange follow-up. Mark actual risk-benefit discussion and communication, not only recall of one memorized numeric threshold.

**Interview challenge:** In plain patient-friendly language, explain why you need to confirm the anticoagulant medicine and intended procedure before recommending a plan.

**Reviewer gap:** Full local anaesthesia assessment, full infection/endocarditis/antiresorptive management, precise radiographic technique and detailed postoperative patient leaflet are out of scope of this **pilot** and need their own source-locator review.

## Proposed MCQ revisions — 2 item-replacement candidates, do not update original keys yet

### MCQ OMS-001-Q1 (candidate replaces UUID 6fac07ab-bc4a-463c-8d9d-7e262b9165b1)

**Stem:** An adult arrives for a planned extraction. The tooth has been identified and the patient has agreed to extraction, but the chart contains no updated medical/medication/allergy history or documented evaluation of relevant clinical and radiographic findings. Which action best completes a safe **pre-operative assessment**?

- **A.** Reconfirm identity, tooth/site and indication, update medical/drug/allergy and relevant bleeding-risk history, review appropriate clinical/radiographic findings, discuss alternatives and risks, and ensure valid consent/documentation.
- **B.** Confirm tooth number and written consent alone; investigate systemic risk only if a problem occurs during the procedure.
- **C.** Record systemic medication history only, since the patient's agreement makes a fresh local examination unnecessary.
- **D.** Obtain radiographs for every patient irrespective of indication, then proceed without reconciling current drugs or allergies.

**Provisional single-best answer: A — NOT YET CLINICALLY VERIFIED.**

**Rationale candidate:** IDA Standard 9 expects medical-history and oral assessment, while Standard 10 addresses planning and consent. B omits foreseeable systemic risk; C omits clinical suitability; D falsely treats imaging as universal and ignores medication/allergy safety. The exact wording and all distractors require qualified review for unambiguity, standards currency and India-specific scope.

**Wrong-answer remedial link:** Return to Stage A and B; require a *new* unseen context question for mastery.

### MCQ OMS-001-Q2 (candidate replaces UUID dc25b46f-5eea-419c-a51b-53009d7cfd17)

**Stem:** An adult patient returns to a dental clinic soon after extraction with **persistent socket oozing despite initial pressure**. They are currently alert, without evident airway compromise, but medicine and bleeding history have not yet been reconciled. Which approach best balances immediate assessment and local management, while recognizing the need for escalation?

- **A.** Assess bleeding severity and systemic stability, clarify risk factors/medication, apply appropriate local measures and urgently reassess the source/response; escalate if bleeding remains uncontrolled or the patient deteriorates.
- **B.** Wait for an INR result before undertaking any local assessment, even when anticoagulant class has not been established.
- **C.** Instruct the patient to stop all anticoagulant and antiplatelet medicines immediately and defer checking the bleeding site until the next day.
- **D.** Place sutures without assessing bleeding severity, source, underlying medication or systemic state; assume this always resolves haemorrhage.

**Provisional single-best answer: A — NOT YET CLINICALLY VERIFIED.**

**Rationale candidate:** The March 2026 SDCEP post-extraction haemorrhage/triage resources distinguish bleeding patterns, prompt local care and escalation. B wrongly assumes INR is relevant for every anticoagulant and delays assessment; C introduces potentially serious thrombosis risk and delayed care; D omits assessment and patient-specific judgment. This is a **clinician-facing educational scenario**, not a patient management instruction. Do not show answer/rationale in exam mode before submission.

**Wrong-answer remedial link:** Return to Stages C and D and supply a separate fresh case contrasting a stable presentation with emergency red flags.

## Proposed anticoagulant branching case — still no one-size-fits-all treatment decision

**Case identity:** 9ca96a5b-6cc9-43e1-b7fe-9346dcf101e2, candidate v0.2  
**Initial vignette:** An adult requests extraction and reports taking “a blood thinner.” No exact medicine, indication, regimen or procedural complexity is yet documented.

**Step 1 — What is missing?** Ask for agent(s), indication, last dose, accompanying antiplatelets/other medicines and interactions, bleeding/thrombotic history, relevant renal/hepatic/cardiovascular disease, allergy, dental presentation, intended extraction complexity and care access. Passing this step requires saying “insufficient information to decide”, not guessing.

**Step 2 — Conditional fork (distinct scenarios; do not combine them):**
- **VKA branch:** The dentist is informed that the person takes a named VKA. Ask which current INR information and clinical factors would be relevant *under SDCEP's warfarin/VKA pathway*, and why the result alone cannot substitute for overall assessment. The guideline's 24/72-hour INR timing and <4 threshold require case, date and local applicability review; they are not universal prescriptions.
- **DOAC branch:** The dentist is informed that the person takes apixaban. Ask why warfarin INR logic does not apply and which drug/procedure factors and current guidance must be checked before any management decision.
- **Combination branch:** The dentist learns there are anticoagulant and antiplatelet drugs together. Ask when specialized/prescriber collaboration or referral may be needed; avoid a fabricated automatic interruption plan.

**Step 3 — Procedural and safety context:** Clarify simple vs surgical/multiple extraction, expected tissue trauma, site, bleeding-control capacity, medical complexity and monitoring. If severe symptoms or bleeding are present, prioritize urgent/emergency assessment rather than a routine planning algorithm.

**Step 4 — Patient-facing communication:** Explain medical uncertainty, the need to coordinate treatment safely, informed consent and individualized follow-up without assuring that a medicine must always be continued or held.

**Scoring rubric candidate (NOT activated):**
- Names required missing information and class-specific anticoagulant distinctions.
- Does not equate DOAC with VKA INR monitoring.
- Does not advise unqualified stopping/bridging of therapy.
- Identifies bleeding/systemic deterioration red flags.
- Communicates uncertainty and documentation/referral.
- Requires an independent dentist to establish defensible keyed reasoning, pass/fail criteria and remediation wording before grading any real learner.

**Interactive scaffolding proposal:** Provide 2 graded hints before showing a source-grounded feedback explanation; after review add distinct unseen case variants and 1/7/30-day spaced probes. Merely repeating the same stem must not count as retained mastery.
